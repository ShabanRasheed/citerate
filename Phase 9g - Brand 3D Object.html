Why this and not a logo render

A 3D object earns its place only if it carries an idea. Each of these encodes one: the mark, the wordmark, the diagnosis, and the instrument. None of them is decoration with a bevel on it.

Base colours are lifted a step from the screen palette because the studio rig has no environment map — metalness above ~0.4 would render near-black.

Brand rules this obeys
  • The slash is always the cited green — never red or amber. Those hues carry data meaning.
  • Clear space around the mark equals the slash's own thickness, in three dimensions as on screen.
  • The fourth cause segment is slatted rather than solid, because texture — not hue — is what makes it survive greyscale.
  • No gradient, no glow, no chrome. The material story is matte ink, one green, one machined grey.
Where these get used
  • Mark tile — app store listing, favicon master, sticker die-line, 3D-printed desk object.
  • Monolith — pitch-deck cover render, conference backdrop, office reception piece.
  • Network — the /how-it-works hero and the diagram on the method page.
  • Instrument — the "Clinical Readout" identity made literal; good for a launch film's opening shot.

Every mesh and material is named, so the OBJ and GLB arrive in Blender with a usable part hierarchy rather than one welded blob.
