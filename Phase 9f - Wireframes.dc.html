WHY A DRAWER, NOT A PAGE

P1 opens six to ten of these per session. A route per query would mean six to ten back-navigations, each losing her filter and scroll position. The drawer keeps the app two levels deep, permanently.

Frames 08–09 · act & compare

Fix queue, and the competitor pane at Starter tier

08 · /FIXES

One card expanded, the rest collapsed to a title and a status pill — the queue is a list of one decision at a time, not a wall of options. Estimated recovery sits top-right with its band; effort class beneath it.

THE TWO CONTROLS

Filled: mark shipped. Outlined: copy for ticket. The dashed strip below them is the latency disclosure — "verification needs two scans, expect a verdict by the 9th" — visible before the click, not after (US-25).

09 · /COMPETITORS AT STARTER

The most important wireframe in the set commercially. Five rows of the user's real result — correct bar lengths, correct counts — blurred, with the unlock card centred on top. Nothing here is fabricated; hiding the names is the only thing the gate does.

RULE THIS FRAME ENCODES

Locked ≠ hidden, and locked ≠ a marketing page. The upsell copy names the specific unlock in the user's own numbers, and after checkout they return to this exact scroll position, unblurred (US-38).

Coverage check

What is not wireframed, and why that is fine

STRUCTURALLY IDENTICAL

/settings profile, sessions, email, brand, workspaces and /reports index all reuse frame 11's tab-plus-list shell. Redrawing them would produce five near-identical pictures and zero new decisions.

CONTENT PAGES

Blog, docs, changelog, legal and compare pages are a single prose template at --max-prose width. They were resolved in Phase 6d as type specimens, which is the right medium for them — a wireframe of a text page tells you nothing.

STATES, NOT SCREENS

Loading, empty, partial, error, locked and over-quota are annotated on the frames they affect rather than drawn as separate pictures — because that is how they are built, and separating them is how teams forget to build them.
