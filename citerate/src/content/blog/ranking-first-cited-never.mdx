## What we measured

We took 200 domains across eight categories, each holding a top-three organic position on at least ten commercial queries. For every domain we ran its own top ten queries through four engines, three runs each — 24,000 answer runs in total. Then we asked one question of the results: when you already win the link, do you also win the answer?

The short version is that position and citation are only loosely related. Winning the blue link tells you surprisingly little about whether an engine will name you when someone asks the same thing conversationally.

## The 24% blind spot

Fifty-one of the 200 domains — just over a quarter — recorded a citation rate of zero on their own winning queries. Not low. Zero. These are sites with authority, traffic, and a rank tracker showing green.

> Every one of those fifty-one domains had a dashboard that said everything was fine.

The pattern in the failures was consistent: pages written to rank, not to be quoted. Long preambles, buried specifics, no stated numbers, and comparisons that carefully avoid naming an alternative.

## Which queries fail

Failure clusters by intent, not by topic. Comparison and alternatives queries were the worst performers across every category we scanned; how-to queries were the most forgiving.

| Intent cluster | Cited | n |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How-to / informational | 61% | 6,120 |
| Category pick ("best X for Y") | 38% | 7,440 |
| Pricing | 33% | 4,080 |
| Alternatives / vs | 19% | 6,360 |

## What the winners do

The domains cited above 60% shared three traits, and none of them are exotic: they state specific numbers on the page, they name their alternatives explicitly, and they answer the question in the first two sentences rather than the eighth paragraph.

That's not a growth hack. It's the same advice technical writers have given for thirty years, now with a measurable consequence attached.
