## The shape that gets quoted

A page an engine can cite has to be a page an engine can *quote*. In practice that means the specific claim, the number, and the name all appear close together and early.

The three changes that moved the needle most, in order:

1. **State the numbers.** "Starts at $49/month for 100 tracked queries" is citable. "Flexible pricing to suit your needs" is not.
2. **Name the alternatives.** Pages that name competitors get cited on alternatives queries. Pages that say "other tools" do not.
3. **Answer in the first two sentences.** Preamble is where citations go to die.

## The three habits that keep you invisible

Writing for a keyword rather than a question. Hiding specifics behind a form. And treating a comparison page as a sales asset that must never admit a rival exists — which is precisely the page an engine will skip in favour of a review site that will.

## What barely mattered

Adding schema markup produced a median lift of 3 points, well inside the confidence band for most pages in the sample. It is cheap and worth doing, but it is not the lever. We would rather say that than sell you a checklist.
