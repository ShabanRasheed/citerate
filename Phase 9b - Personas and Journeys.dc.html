DO
P1 · Primary · the diagnostician

Dana Okafor — 34, Senior SEO Manager, Northline (B2B SaaS)

Eight years in organic search, four of them in-house. Owns a 4 000-page content library and reports to a VP who reads one slide. Technically fluent: writes her own regex in GSC, can read a JSON-LD blob, will not accept a metric she cannot explain the derivation of.

"Rankings are flat, impressions are flat, and sessions are down 22%. I have been asked three times what happened and I have said 'AI answers, probably' three times. Probably is not a finding."
GOALS
  • Attribute the drop to a named cause with a number attached
  • Ship 3–5 page changes a sprint that a dev won't push back on
  • Walk into the quarterly review with a defensible series
FRUSTRATIONS
  • Tools that report a proprietary 0–100 "AI score" with no method page
  • Being handed 400 "opportunities" with no ordering
  • Week-over-week wobble presented as a trend
BEHAVIOURS
  • Opens the tool Tuesday and Thursday, 25–40 minutes
  • Builds saved views per topic cluster and never uses the default
  • Exports to CSV to paste into the dev ticket
SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

A fix-proof report showing citation rate on 41 pricing-cluster queries moving 12% → 29%, band-separated, dated either side of a deploy. She forwards the PDF, not a screenshot.

SCENARIO · TUESDAY 09:40, THE WEEK AFTER A BAD BOARD MEETING
09:41
Opens the Monday digest she left unread. Score 58 ±5, down 4 from last week — the email says within noise, so she doesn't panic. What she does notice is the line beneath: pricing cluster −11, outside band. She clicks that line, not the header.
09:43
Lands on /queries pre-filtered to that cluster, sorted by delta. Eleven rows red. The cause column reads AIO displacement on nine of them and other / unexplained on two. She trusts the tool slightly more for the two.
09:47
Expands the worst row. The drawer shows 2/18 cited runs, the engine grid with two SURFACE and two PROXY labels, and the three competitor hosts cited instead. One is a competitor's comparison page published in March. That is the moment she stops doubting.
09:55
Jumps to /fixes. Top item is not the AIO cluster — it's a technical-decay fix on a template that blocks the crawler on 60 pages, worth an estimated 34 recoverable citations at low effort. She reorders her sprint on the spot and copies the fix into a ticket.
Thu 16:20
Deploy lands. She marks the fix shipped and the queue tells her verification needs two scans, arriving Saturday. No fake instant confirmation.
+11 days
Fix flips to verified: 12% → 29%, bands don't overlap. She generates a fix-proof report and pastes the link in the exec channel. This is the first time anyone has replied to one of her posts.

MV
P2 · Primary · the time-poor operator

Marco Vidal — 41, Founder & CEO, Tallyroom (6 people)

Technical founder who does marketing between support tickets and payroll. $340k ARR, growing on content he wrote himself two years ago. Knows enough SEO to be dangerous, has no time to become good at it. Buys tools on a 90-second decision and cancels anything he hasn't opened in a month.

"I don't want a dashboard. I asked ChatGPT for the best tool in my category and it named three competitors and not me. Just tell me if that's a pattern and what to change."
GOALS
  • Know in under two minutes whether he has a problem
  • Get one change per month he can do himself in an hour
  • Never think about this again between emails
FRUSTRATIONS
  • Onboarding wizards that ask for things he'd have to look up
  • Tools that require a login to see the answer he already paid for
  • Being upsold before being shown anything
BEHAVIOURS
  • Reads the digest on a phone, standing up
  • Skips onboarding steps that offer a skip
  • Judges the whole product by the free scan's first sentence
SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

Month four, he has opened the app twice, done two fixes from the email, and his rate went 9% → 22%. He renews without thinking about it — the definition of retention for this segment.

PR
P3 · Primary · the reseller

Priya Raman — 38, Owner, Halfstack SEO (12 staff, 14 retainers)

Runs a boutique agency on $4k–$18k monthly retainers. Her margin is her team's hours, and the last week of every month currently disappears into reporting decks. Two clients asked about AI answers this quarter; she answered honestly that she was looking into it, and hated how that sounded.

"I need a chart in my template, with my logo, that says what we changed and what it moved. If your brand is on the PDF I can't send it — and if the number restates itself next month, I've handed a client a reason to distrust me."
GOALS
  • Add an AI-visibility line item to every retainer without adding hours
  • Cut monthly reporting from 4 hours per client to minutes
  • Walk into renewals with proof, not narrative
FRUSTRATIONS
  • Per-seat pricing that punishes her for having a team
  • Tools that make her clients log in to a fourth dashboard
  • Numbers that change retroactively after she's presented them
BEHAVIOURS
  • Works in a 2-hour block in the last week of the month
  • Switches client context 14 times in that block
  • Gives clients a client-role seat, read-only, or no seat at all
SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

She raises two retainers by $800/mo on the strength of a new deliverable that costs her 20 minutes a month, and quotes the fix-proof report in a renewal she expected to lose.

SCENARIO · THE LAST THURSDAY OF THE MONTH, 14:00–15:40
14:02
Opens /reports. Fourteen monthly snapshots were frozen automatically on the 1st, each already carrying its method version. Nothing to generate; the work is review, not production.
14:06
One client's rate dropped 6 points. She checks the band before she panics: bands overlap, so the readout labels it within noise and she does not put it on a slide. This is the single most valuable thing the product does for her credibility.
14:20
Another client — the one at renewal risk — has a verified fix from week two: 14% → 31% across nine queries. She opens the fix-proof snapshot, confirms the dates straddle the deploy, and downloads the white-label PDF. Her logo, her accent colour, one immovable footer line referencing the methodology.
14:35
Cycles the workspace switcher through the remaining twelve. Two need a sentence of context typed into the report note; the rest ship as-is. Nine of fourteen reports leave without her editing anything.
15:10
A quota banner: one client is 140 queries over. She buys two overage blocks from /settings/billing without leaving the flow. The scan was never interrupted, so there's no hole in that client's series.
15:38
Done in 96 minutes for 14 clients. Previously: two working days. She adds the deliverable to her pitch deck the same week.

ES
P4 · Secondary · shapes v2, not v1

Elena Sørensen — 45, Director of Organic Growth, regulated enterprise

Will never open the dashboard. A delegate pulls the number into a board pack via the read API. Her involvement is procurement: DPA, sub-processor list, SSO/SAML, data residency, an audit trail of who exported what. She is listed here because the absence of her requirements is a v1 decision we should make on purpose rather than discover in a sales call.

SHIPPED IN V1 FOR HER
  • /security and /legal/[slug] as real sales assets
  • Read API on Growth+, read-write on Scale+
  • Session list with revoke, per user
  • Immutable snapshots — the audit story starts here
KNOWINGLY DEFERRED
  • SSO / SAML and SCIM provisioning
  • 2FA enrolment (out of scope in Phase 8 onboarding by decision)
  • Export audit log and data-residency choice
  • Contractual SLA and uptime page
WHY DEFERRING IS SAFE

Enterprise is priced "talk to us" precisely so the first two or three deals can be served with a bespoke contract and manual provisioning. Building SSO before a signed logo is building for a persona who hasn't paid yet. Revisit the moment a deal stalls on it twice.

✕ · Anti-persona

Who we refuse to design for

"Ravi" — growth hacker chasing an AI-visibility number to put on a slide

Wants one big score, going up, weekly, with no method page and no error bars. Would happily accept a made-up cause for every query. Would love a leaderboard. Would churn the first month the number honestly went sideways.

WHAT SERVING HIM WOULD COST US
  • Dropping the confidence band, which is the only reason Priya can send a report
  • Removing the unexplained segment, which is the only reason Dana believes the other three
  • Weekly "wins" emails, which is exactly why Marco unsubscribes from other tools

Every feature request that reduces to "make the number simpler and friendlier" gets tested against this card first.
