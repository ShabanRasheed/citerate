HOW THIS PACKAGE FITS THE PROJECT

Phases 1–6 designed the product. Phase 7 built the marketing site, Phase 8 built the dashboard. Phase 9 is the layer that was missing between them: the documentation that lets somebody who wasn't in the room understand why the built thing is shaped this way — and lets you defend it to an investor, hand it to a developer, or put it in a portfolio.

Nothing here is invented after the fact to look tidy. Every route name, plan limit, scoring rule and database table referenced across these boards is read from the two repos, so the documentation and the code cannot drift apart silently.

What comes next

Phases 10 to 12, in the order that gets you launched

Phase 10 is not optional and not parallelisable — four placeholders currently stand between a complete codebase and a product that can take money. Everything else waits behind them.

PHASE 10 · CRITICAL PATH
blocks launch
Close the four placeholders
01 · the 25-query generator

The highest-leverage of the four: it determines whether every number in the product feels real. Bad seed queries make a correct measurement worthless.

02 · Paddle price IDs

Currently returning 501. Six plans, six add-ons, monthly and yearly — plus the webhook that flips the plan and the meter that bills overage.

03 · the PDF renderer

Without it there is no white-label deliverable, and without that there is no Agency tier — which is the whole distribution strategy in 9h.

04 · Search Console OAuth callback

The smallest job, and it unblocks cause separation — without GSC more queries fall into "unexplained" than they should.

Also in scope: the six lib files duplicated across the two repos should become one shared package, so citation formats and the token system stop being maintained twice.

PHASE 11
before first paying user
Hardening & launch readiness
  • The 24-check accessibility list in 9h, run on all 48 routes — check 21 first.
  • Every state in the definition of done actually built: loading, empty, partial, error, locked, over-quota, within-noise, zero-cited.
  • Email deliverability: SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and a seed test of the digest with images off.
  • Seed the first ten fix templates and instrument verified-fix rate from day one — 9h names it as the metric that decides whether the loop closes.
  • Load and cost modelling for a 2 000-query workspace on daily refresh, since runs-per-query is the whole unit economic.
  • Legal pages real, not placeholder: terms, privacy, DPA, sub-processor list.
  • Onboarding instrumentation on the F02 email round trip — the largest drop-off in the funnel.
PHASE 12
first 90 days
Go to market & learn
  • Launch assets from this package: the 9g object as the pitch cover, 9a's loop as the explainer, 9b's scenarios as the sales narrative.
  • Ten design-partner agencies before any paid acquisition — 9h's distribution argument is that one Priya carries the product into fourteen companies.
  • Watch three numbers: free-scan completion, F02 claim conversion, and verified-fix rate. Everything else is vanity at this stage.
  • Revisit the deferred list only on evidence: SSO on the second stalled enterprise deal, scheduled report delivery once domain authentication is solved, CMS auto-fixes never before the fix corpus is trustworthy.
  • Publish the first method-version change transparently, with a flagged discontinuity. Doing it once, well, is worth more than any trust page.

Optional, and cheap given what exists: a Phase 13 investor deck assembled almost entirely from these eight boards — the thesis, the loop, the personas, the landscape and the 3D object are already the five slides that matter.
