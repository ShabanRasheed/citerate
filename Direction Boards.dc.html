1Phase 1 — brand direction boards

Two divergent directions, both honoring the §2 invariants (rates with run counts, surface/proxy tags, "other/unexplained" segment, confidence bands, mono-for-data). A evolves the existing lab-report prototype: same bones, sharpened into a real specimen-document language. B replaces the sans display with a serif "clinical readout" voice — calmer, more editorial, closer to "a good doctor reading results," and harder to mistake for a generic SaaS template. Both keep color paired with text/shape signals and work on white for the white-label PDF.

1aDirection A — Lab Report, evolved
Direction A · evolves the prototype
Lab Report

The prototype's strongest ideas — paper ground, ink, mono-for-data — pushed all the way into a specimen-document language: hairline rules, uppercase mono microlabels on every data region, square corners, a ruled score unit. Feels like an instrument printout you'd trust, not a dashboard. Why keep it: IBM Plex Sans/Mono are cut from the same family, so UI and data feel like one voice; the palette already clears AA on paper and white.

1bDirection B — Clinical Readout (alternative)
Direction B · alternative
Clinical Readout

Verdicts are sentences, so the display face is a serif — the voice of a written medical opinion, not a metric widget. Warm paper, forest/brick semantics, JetBrains Mono for every number. UI chrome runs on the system sans stack (zero font cost → faster LCP; the two webfont families are spent on serif + mono). Warmer and more human than A; the risk is it reads more editorial than instrument — mono microlabels keep it technical.
