01 · Positioning

02 · The five categories

Who the buyer is comparing us to, and what each does well

Written as behaviour, not brands. If a sales conversation stalls, it stalls on one of these five — and the "does well" column is not diplomacy, it is the part we must not pretend away.

Category
Does genuinely well
Structural gap
Our line in the room
CAT A
Rank trackers & enterprise SEO suites
Deep crawl diagnostics, keyword universes, mature reporting, and remediation advice built over a decade. Already in the stack and already trusted.
They measure position on a results page. When the answer replaces the results page, position is intact and the traffic is gone — which is exactly the situation P1 cannot explain with these tools.
"Keep them. We answer the question their charts can't: was the click taken by an answer, and by whom."
CAT B
AI-visibility dashboards
Correct instinct and the right unit. Fast to market, often good at breadth of engines.
A score with no denominator, no interval, and no cause. The user learns they moved from 58 to 54 and cannot tell whether that is a problem, let alone what to do — so the tool becomes a screenshot in a slide and then a cancelled subscription.
"Ask them two things: over how many runs, and what is the confidence interval. Then ask what you should change on Monday."
CAT C
Brand-mention & share-of-voice monitors
Genuinely useful for a comms or PR team. Broad source coverage, sentiment, alerting maturity.
Being named is not being linked. A mention with no citation recovers no traffic, and the buyer is a communications lead rather than an organic-growth owner.
"Different job, different buyer. We measure the link, not the conversation."
CAT D
Consultants & one-off audits
The best cause attribution available, because a human reads the pages. Highly persuasive deliverable.
A photograph, not a series. It cannot verify the fix it recommended, and repeating it monthly is unaffordable — which is why P3 wants to become this category with our instrument underneath.
"An audit tells you what was true in March. We tell you what is true this week, and whether your fix worked."
CAT E
Typing prompts in by hand
Free, instant, requires no procurement, and the answer feels completely convincing. This is what the overwhelming majority actually do.
n = 1. Ask the same engine the same question five times and get five different answers — so a hand check produces a confident belief with no evidential value, in either direction.
"You already did this once and it scared you. We did it 1 200 times and can tell you whether it was real."
THE UNCOMFORTABLE ONE

Category E is the real competitor and the free scan exists entirely to beat it. It meets the hand-check on its own terms — no signup, an answer in twenty seconds — and then does the one thing a hand check structurally cannot: repeat itself enough times to say whether the result was signal. Every second of latency in F01 is a point of market share against Category E, not against Category B.

03 · Defensibility

Five things that get harder to copy over time

01 · ACCUMULATING
The fix-outcome corpus

Every verified fix and every "no measurable change" re-weights the templates. After a year of shipped changes and their measured outcomes, the prioritisation is trained on evidence nobody else holds. This is the only compounding asset in the product.

02 · SWITCHING COST
The unbroken series

A time series cannot be backfilled. Eighteen months of runs on the same query set, under versioned methods, is not portable — which is also precisely why we must never let a billing event punch a hole in it.

03 · REPUTATIONAL
Published, versioned method

A public method page, immutable snapshots and flagged discontinuities are cheap to build and expensive to start doing — a competitor who has quietly restated numbers cannot retroactively become trustworthy.

04 · DISTRIBUTION
Agencies as the channel

One Agency seat carries the instrument into fourteen companies under someone else's brand. White-label makes P3 a reseller with no commission structure to negotiate — and she will not re-onboard fourteen clients onto a rival for a marginal feature.

05 · CULTURAL
Willingness to say "we don't know"

The weakest-looking feature is the hardest to copy, because copying it means a competitor's own dashboard starts admitting uncertainty. Most organisations cannot bring themselves to ship that.

Compresses the category into a line item on an invoice the buyer already pays.
A bundled feature will report a score, not a verified cause — the same gap as Category B, now with distribution. Compete on the fix-outcome corpus and the agency channel, never on breadth.

Terminal for the unit of measurement.
Currently moving the other way — citation is the trust mechanism these products rely on. Watch citation density per answer as a leading indicator; it is already worth tracking as an internal metric.
MED
Fixes stop moving the number
If verified fixes are rare, stage 5 never closes and the loop is a dashboard after all.
The single metric to instrument from day one: verified-fix rate. If it is under ~30%, the fix templates are the problem to solve, not the UI.
HIGH
Honesty reads as weakness in sales
A rival's confident 0–100 score out-demos an interval and a striped unknown segment.
Sell the interval as the feature: it is what lets a buyer present the number without risk. If it keeps losing demos, fix the framing, never the statistics.
MED
Scan cost per run outruns the price ladder
2 000 queries × several engines × several runs is the whole unit economic. Daily refresh multiplies it.
Runs per query are a tunable, not a constant. Report the denominator honestly and reduce runs on stable queries rather than raising prices or quietly narrowing the sample.
HIGH
Enterprise deals stall on SSO and residency
P4's requirements are knowingly deferred; two lost deals in a row makes that a mistake rather than a decision.
Track the stall reason on every Enterprise conversation. Build SSO on the second stall, not the first request — and never before a signed logo.

05 · Accessibility specification

WCAG 2.2 AA, written as build rules

This product's whole value is a finding being legible. A chart that only works in colour, or a drawer that traps a keyboard user, is not an accessibility oversight here — it is a failure of the core proposition.

CONTRAST · TOKEN BY TOKEN
--ink on --card14.9:1body, all sizes
--ink-soft on --card6.4:1secondary text, all sizes
--cited-ink on --cardAApositive figures at 12px+
--gap-ink on --cardAAnegative figures at 12px+
--warn2.6:1never for text — fills only. Use --warn-ink.
--ink-muted≥3:1non-text only: rules, icons, marks
--side-ink-muted on --side-bg5.5:1sidebar secondary labels

The lifted dark-surface set (--side-cited, --side-gap…) exists because the light semantics fail on ink. Never reuse the light values on the sidebar.

NON-COLOUR ENCODING · 1.4.1

Every data mark carries a second channel. This is enforced in the design system, not left to the implementer.

Cause 1 — solid + always labelled in the legend
Cause 2 — solid + label
Cause 3 — solid + label
Cause 4 — always striped, in every medium, at every size
SURFACEFilled pill
PROXYDashed outline — distinguishable with no colour at all

Deltas pair the colour with a sign and an arrow glyph. Fix statuses pair colour with a word: open · shipped · verified · no measurable change.

KEYBOARD · 2.1.1 · 2.4.3 · 2.4.7
  • Skip link to main, first in tab order on every route.
  • Query rows are focusable; Enter opens the evidence drawer, Esc closes it and returns focus to that row.
  • The drawer is role="dialog" with focus trapped while open.
  • Workspace switcher is a real combobox: type-ahead, arrow keys, Esc to cancel.
  • Focus ring is the two-tone token (--focus-ring) so it survives on both paper and ink. Never outline:none without a replacement.
  • No keyboard trap in the PDF preview iframe.
SCREEN READER · 1.3.1 · 4.1.2 · 4.1.3
  • Every chart has a text equivalent that states the same finding — the verdict sentence usually is it, so associate them rather than writing a second description.
  • Rates are announced as "11 of 60 runs, 18 percent", not "11/60".
  • The cause bar is a list of four labelled items, not an unlabelled group of divs.
  • Scan progress and toast messages go through one polite live region; a completed scan announces once.
  • Table headers are real th with scope; sort state uses aria-sort.
  • Locked regions announce "locked — upgrade to see competitor names", never just visual blur.
MOTION, TARGETS & INPUT · 2.3.3 · 2.5.8
  • prefers-reduced-motion zeroes all five duration tokens; state still changes instantly, so nothing feels disabled.
  • The count-up on the score is decoration — the final value is in the DOM from the first frame.
  • Nothing flashes, nothing auto-scrolls, nothing moves on hover except a 1px border.
  • Hit targets ≥ 24 × 24 px everywhere, ≥ 44 × 44 px on any touch layout.
  • Chart segments are clickable at ≥ 24px in their smaller dimension, or paired with a legend row that is.
  • No drag-only interaction: the threshold slider accepts arrow keys and a typed number.
FORMS & ERRORS · 3.3.1 · 3.3.2 · 3.3.7
  • Every field has a visible persistent label. Placeholders are never the label.
  • Errors are text next to the field, programmatically associated, and they name the remedy — not just the fault.
  • The 6-digit code field is one input with autocomplete="one-time-code", not six separate boxes that fight paste and screen readers. The wireframe draws six boxes; the build must not.
  • Nothing already provided is re-asked: the claim flow carries the email and the domain forward (3.3.7).
  • Destructive actions — revoke a session, remove the last admin, downgrade — state the consequence in the confirmation, in the user's own numbers.
PRINT, EMAIL & ZOOM · 1.4.4 · 1.4.10
  • PDF reports must be readable in greyscale: this is the real reason segment four is striped, and it is checked on every report change.
  • The digest email works with images off and in a text-only client — the verdict sentence is text, never an image.
  • Reflows to 320px with no horizontal scrolling; wide tables scroll within their own container, not the page.
  • Legible at 200% zoom with no loss of function; the sidebar collapses to the 64px rail rather than overlapping content.
  • Minimum on-screen data size is 11.5px mono (--t-data-sm) and 12pt in print.
06 · Pre-launch checklist

Twenty-four checks, run on every route before Phase 10 closes

AUTOMATED · EVERY BUILD
  1. axe-core clean on all 26 app + 22 marketing routes
  2. Contrast assertion over every token pair actually used
  3. No outline:none without a replacement ring
  4. Every img and svg has alt text or aria-hidden
  5. Heading order has no skipped level
  6. Every form control resolves to a label
  7. Lang attribute present; page title unique per route
  8. No rate rendered without its run count (product invariant, tested)
MANUAL · EVERY RELEASE
  1. Complete F02 claim flow by keyboard only
  2. Complete F10 fix lifecycle by keyboard only
  3. Open and close six evidence drawers; focus returns correctly each time
  4. Switch workspace with the keyboard; verify full re-scope
  5. VoiceOver pass on /overview and /queries
  6. NVDA pass on /fixes and /reports/[id]
  7. Reduced-motion on: nothing animates, everything still changes
  8. 320px width: no horizontal page scroll on any route
PRODUCT-SPECIFIC · THE ONES THAT MATTER MOST
  1. Print every report kind in greyscale; all four cause segments still distinguishable
  2. Digest email with images disabled still states the finding
  3. Locked regions announce their lock, not just blur it
  4. 200% zoom on /queries: table scrolls in its container, sidebar rails
  5. Colour-blindness simulation (deuteranopia + achromatopsia) on the cause bar and every delta
  6. Within-noise state announces "within noise", not a bare number
  7. Partial-scan state exposes the reduced denominator to assistive tech
  8. All six settings panes reachable and saveable with no pointer
Check 21 is the one to run first. If the cause bar fails a colour-blindness simulation, the product's central diagnostic is unreadable to roughly one in twelve men — and no amount of copy repairs a chart that cannot be seen.
