Method

One query, followed all the way down.

A score you can't explain is a score you can't defend. So instead of listing features, this page follows a single real query — "best crm for startups" — through the four stages of every scan, showing the actual rule applied at each step. At the end: what the method cannot see, stated plainly.

1Stage one

Build the prompt set

Buyers don't search keywords at AI engines — they ask questions. We propose a set from three sources: your site's pages, your Search Console queries if connected, and question patterns from your category. Each prompt gets an intent and a cluster label; clusters are what the Fix Queue later groups on.

You edit everything. A tool that writes its own exam and grades itself proves nothing — the set is yours, and it stays visible on every report.

2Stage two

Ask every engine, more than once

The same question gets different answers on different days — sometimes in the same hour. One run is an anecdote. Each refresh asks every engine the same prompt three times and records the runs separately; the citation rate is computed over runs, never over a single lucky answer.

Prompts are fixed-wording, unpersonalized, US-region by default. Raw answer text is stored, so every number on your dashboard can show its receipts.

3Stage three

Match citations by URL, not by vibes

A citation counts when the answer links a URL that resolves to your domain — redirects followed, subdomains included, UTM stripped. A brand mention without a link is recorded as a mention, tracked separately, never inflated into the citation rate.

4Stage four

Attribute the cause — or say "unexplained"

For every uncited query, the evidence is weighed: did an AI answer appear at all, did your ranking hold, is the page technically readable, who got cited instead. The verdict is one of four causes, each with a confidence score.

Below 0.6 confidence, the verdict is unexplained — striped in every chart, never silently folded into a cause. A diagnosis tool that always has a diagnosis is a horoscope.

Stated plainly

What the score cannot see

PersonalizationA logged-in user with chat history may get a different answer. We measure the unpersonalized baseline — the answer a stranger gets.
GeographyDefault is US-region. Answers differ by market; additional regions are a Scale feature, not a silent average.
Model driftEngines change weights without notice. A sudden industry-wide shift shows as a flagged discontinuity on your chart, not as your failure.
Sampling errorThree runs bound, not eliminate, variance. Charts carry confidence bands; week-over-week moves inside the band are labeled as noise.
Every report ends with this section, abbreviated. Buyers of measurement tools have been burned before; the limitations section is a feature.

Compare · Citerate vs doing it yourself

You could do this in a spreadsheet. Here's what that actually takes.

The DIY version is real and we'll describe it fairly: ask each engine your queries, paste the answers into a sheet, tally who got cited. It works. The question is what it costs weekly, and what it can't do at all.

The spreadsheet Citerate
Coverage per weekHowever many queries you have patience for, usually 10–20, one engine100–2,000 queries, every engine in plan, on schedule
Answer varianceOne run per query — you record whichever answer you happened to getThree runs per engine per refresh; rate computed over runs
Personalization bleedYour logged-in account colors every answer — you're measuring your own bubbleUnpersonalized baseline, fixed wording, fixed region
Cause attributionNot possible from answer text alone — needs rank + technical evidence per queryFour causes with confidence, or an honest "unexplained"
History and deltasWhatever tab hygiene survives quarter twoPer-fix before/after, per-query trend, up to unlimited history
Weekly time cost≈ 3–5 hours of copy-paste for 20 queries × 1 engine × 1 runZero — it ran before you sat down
Time estimate assumes manual prompting and tallying; your mileage varies with query count.

When the spreadsheet is actually fine

A one-off curiosity check on five queries? Ask the engines yourself — you don't need us. The spreadsheet stops being fine when you need the same measurement every week, when variance matters, or when someone will challenge the number. If that's not you yet, bookmark this page and go build things.
