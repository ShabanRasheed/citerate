Ranking #1 and cited 0% of the time

We scanned 200 domains holding top-three positions. Fifty-one of them were cited in zero AI answers for the same queries they win on.

Author nameRole · placeholder until bylines are set

What we measured

We took 200 domains across eight categories, each holding a top-three organic position on at least ten commercial queries. For every domain we ran its own top ten queries through four engines, three runs each — 24,000 answer runs in total. Then we asked one question of the results: when you already win the link, do you also win the answer?

The short version is that position and citation are only loosely related. Winning the blue link tells you surprisingly little about whether an engine will name you when someone asks the same thing conversationally.

The 24% blind spot

Fifty-one of the 200 domains — just over a quarter — recorded a citation rate of zero on their own winning queries. Not low. Zero. These are sites with authority, traffic, and a rank tracker showing green.

Every one of those fifty-one domains had a dashboard that said everything was fine.

The pattern in the failures was consistent: pages written to rank, not to be quoted. Long preambles, buried specifics, no stated numbers, and comparisons that carefully avoid naming an alternative.

Which queries fail

Failure clusters by intent, not by topic. Comparison and alternatives queries were the worst performers across every category we scanned; how-to queries were the most forgiving.

What the winners do

The domains cited above 60% shared three traits, and none of them are exotic: they state specific numbers on the page, they name their alternatives explicitly, and they answer the question in the first two sentences rather than the eighth paragraph.

That's not a growth hack. It's the same advice technical writers have given for thirty years, now with a measurable consequence attached.

Limitations

Methodology note 200 domains, 2,000 queries, 4 engines, 3 runs each, US region, unpersonalized, run between Jun 2 and Jun 20, 2026. Categories were not equally weighted — SaaS is over-represented at 34% of the sample. Engine behavior changes without notice, so these figures describe June, not a law of nature. Raw aggregates are available on request.

About

We built the instrument we wanted to be handed.

A measurement product is only worth the trust you place in the people making the measurement. So here's who we are and what we've committed to.

In 2025, the reports stopped making sense. Impressions held, positions held, clicks fell. Every tool on the market drew the same falling line and none of them could say why — because the cause wasn't in the data any of them collected. The answer above the links had started citing three sources, and you were either one of them or you weren't.

Citerate exists to measure that one thing carefully, and to say plainly when it can't. It is deliberately not an AI content generator, not a rank tracker with a new label, and not a dashboard that reframes uncertainty as insight.

What we commit to
01Every number on every screen can show its raw evidence. If we can't show the receipt, we don't show the number.
02Uncertainty is labeled, not smoothed. "Unexplained" stays on the chart, striped, with its real share.
03Your query sets and results are never used to train anything, and never shown to anyone else.
04When our method changes, your history is recomputed and the change is flagged on your chart — not corrected quietly.

Privacy policy

The short version We collect your account details, the domains and queries you ask us to measure, and the results. We use cookieless analytics, so there is no advertising profile to sell and nothing to consent to beyond what the product needs. We never train models on your data and never show your results to another customer. Summary is not the agreement — the sections below are.

1. Who we are

Citerate is operated by [legal entity name], registered at [address]. For privacy questions, write to privacy@citerate.com. Where this policy refers to "you", it means the account holder and, where applicable, the workspace they belong to.

2. What we collect

Account data: name, email, workspace membership, authentication events and trusted devices. Product data: domains, query sets, scan results, raw engine answer text, findings and their states. Billing data is held by our merchant of record; we store only the plan, status, and last four digits.

CategoryPurposeRetention
Account dataAccess, security, supportLife of account + 30d
Scan resultsThe product itselfPer plan history limit
Raw answer textCitation evidence, audits13 months
Free scan resultsServing your share link90 days, then deleted

3. How we use it

To run the scans you ask for, to show you the results, to bill you, and to keep the service secure. We also compute aggregate, de-identified statistics across accounts for published research — never in a form that identifies a domain or a customer. You can opt out of aggregate research in workspace settings.

4. Who we share it with

Only the subprocessors we depend on to operate: infrastructure and storage, transactional email, payment processing, and bot protection. Each is listed with its purpose and location on the subprocessors page, which is versioned — material additions are announced 30 days in advance.

5. Your rights

Access, correction, export, deletion, and objection, exercisable from workspace settings or by writing to privacy@citerate.com. We respond within 30 days. Export is machine-readable, includes raw evidence, and does not require a support conversation.
